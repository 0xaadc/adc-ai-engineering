# 04. Reasoning Models and Test-Time Compute

> As of October 2026. Tags: **(GA)** generally available · **(Preview)** beta/preview · **(⚠ verify)** not confirmed against primary sources.

## Where this fits

This is the model thread, 2024–2025. [§03](03-inference.md) showed that a transformer spends fixed compute per token: one forward pass, whether the token is "the" or the last digit of a hard sum. The only way to spend more compute on a harder problem is to generate more tokens. A reasoning model is trained with reinforcement learning (RL) to do exactly that: it writes a long internal "thinking" passage before answering. This section runs from chain-of-thought prompting (2022) through OpenAI o1 (Sep 2024) and DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025) to today's API controls, and explains why the same recipe made agents reliable in 2025.

## Why this exists

**The problem is fixed compute per token.** Multi-digit arithmetic, tracing a bug through three functions or planning a migration takes many serial steps. If the answer must appear in the first output token, all of that work has to fit in one forward pass, and often it can't.

**The escape hatch is autoregression (P1).** Every emitted token is fed back as input, so writing intermediate steps buys another forward pass per token and leaves a scratchpad that later steps can attend to. More tokens really does mean more computation.

**Chain of thought exploited this.** *Chain of thought (CoT)* means having the model write intermediate reasoning before its answer. Wei et al. (Jan 2022) showed that eight worked examples lifted a 540B-parameter model to state-of-the-art accuracy on GSM8K, a benchmark of grade-school math word problems. Kojima et al. (May 2022) found that "Let's think step by step" worked with no examples at all. But prompted CoT is imitation: the model copies the *form* of reasoning, nothing checks step 3, and it has no trained habit of backing out of dead ends.

**Reasoning became a scaling axis.** *Train-time compute* is what you spend before release; *test-time compute* is what you spend per query. Snell et al. (Aug 2024) found that allocating test-time compute well per prompt was more than 4× more efficient than best-of-N. In a FLOPs-matched comparison it could beat a 14× larger model on problems the small model could already partly solve. "Think longer" joined "train bigger" as a dial, and the open question became how to *train* a model to think well.

## How it works (under the hood)

### RL with verifiable rewards

RLHF ([§02](02-how-llms-work.md)) scores outputs with a learned *reward model*, which the policy can learn to game. Reasoning training replaces the learned judge with a program. *Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR)*, a name from the Tülu 3 paper (Nov 2024), trains on problems a computer can check: math with known answers, code with unit tests.

```text
problem with a checkable answer (math, code + tests)
        │
        ▼
policy model ── samples G attempts ──▶ [<think> … </think> answer] × G
                                                │
                          verifier: is the answer right? do the tests pass?
                                                │  rewards r1 … rG
                                                ▼
                          advantage_i = (r_i − mean(r)) / std(r)
                                                │
                                                ▼
        raise the probability of tokens in above-average attempts, lower it
        for below-average ones, with a KL "leash" to the starting model
```

This works for three reasons. A test passes or it doesn't, so there's no neural judge to flatter. Checkable problems can be generated by the million. And because only the final answer is scored, any habit that raises the hit rate is reinforced, so backtracking and re-checking emerge because they pay off, not because anyone demonstrated them.

### OpenAI o1 (Sep 2024)

OpenAI's "Learning to reason with LLMs" (Sep 12, 2024) introduced o1-preview, trained with RL to produce a hidden chain of thought before answering. Performance improved with both more RL compute (train-time) and more thinking time (test-time). OpenAI withheld the raw chain of thought, showing ChatGPT users only a summarized version (o1 system card), and withheld the training recipe. Reasoning tokens were billed but invisible, and responses took seconds to minutes.

### DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025)

DeepSeek-R1 (arXiv, Jan 22, 2025) mattered because it was open-weight and published how it was made.

- **R1-Zero ran RL directly on a base model, with no supervised fine-tuning.** Its rewards were rule-based: an *accuracy reward* for the final answer and a *format reward* for keeping the thinking inside `<think>` tags. DeepSeek avoided neural reward models because of reward hacking at scale.
- **The results.** AIME 2024 pass@1 rose from 15.6% to 71.0%, or 86.7% with majority voting. Response length grew on its own during training, and the paper describes an "aha moment" in which the model learned to stop and re-evaluate.
- **R1 itself** added a small "cold-start" supervised dataset and a multi-stage pipeline to fix R1-Zero's poor readability and language mixing.
- **GRPO** (*Group Relative Policy Optimization*) came earlier, from DeepSeekMath (Feb 2024), as a variant of PPO, the standard RLHF algorithm. PPO trains a second "value" model to estimate a baseline. GRPO drops it and uses the mean reward of a group of answers to the same prompt, as in the diagram, which saves a second large model.
- **Distillation.** Six smaller open models were fine-tuned on R1's traces. That makes traces valuable training data, which is one reason closed vendors hide them.

So R1 didn't invent GRPO or the RLVR label. It showed, at scale and in public, that pure RL on verifiable rewards produces long, self-correcting reasoning.

### Serial and parallel test-time compute

*Serial* test-time compute means one sample that thinks longer, as reasoning models do. *Parallel* means many independent samples plus a selection step: a majority vote (*self-consistency*, Wang et al. 2022) or a scorer that picks the best (*best-of-N*). They compose: R1-Zero's 71.0% → 86.7% came from voting on top of thinking. Both have diminishing returns, so the real question is how much is enough.

### Thinking as an API control (Anthropic, Oct 2026)

Claude 3.7 Sonnet (Feb 24, 2025) introduced a fixed budget: `thinking: {"type": "enabled", "budget_tokens": N}`. Claude 4 (May 22, 2025) added thinking with tool use. The current design separates *whether* to think from *how much*:

- **`thinking: {"type": "adaptive"}`** **(GA)**: the model decides per request whether and how long to think. It's also the default when `thinking` is omitted on current models.
- **`output_config: {"effort": ...}`** **(GA)**: soft guidance, from `low` through `medium`, `high` and `xhigh` to `max`. The default is `high` on most models and `medium` on Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Haiku 5.5. Lower effort also means fewer, terser tool calls.
- **`max_tokens`** is the only *hard* cap, and it covers thinking plus text.
- **`budget_tokens`** is deprecated on the 4.6 models and rejected (400) on 4.7 and later, which covers every Oct 2026 model ID.
- **Turning thinking off is model-specific.** `claude-sonnet-5-5` rejects `{"type": "disabled"}`; its lowest setting is `{"type": "between_tools"}`, at `high` effort or below. Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1 can't turn thinking off at all. To get less thinking, lower effort rather than prompting for it.
- **Task budgets** **(Preview)** (`output_config.task_budget`) give the model a visible token budget for a whole agentic loop.

### Reading the response

A thinking response has one or more `thinking` blocks before its `text` blocks:

- **`thinking`** holds a readable summary under `display: "summarized"`, or an empty string under `"omitted"`, the default on every current model. `"updates"` **(Preview)** returns only the progress notes written between tool calls.
- **`signature`** is the *full* reasoning, encrypted. The server decrypts it on the next call, so the model gets its real reasoning back, not the summary. Return it unchanged, along with any `redacted_thinking` blocks (encrypted, safety-redacted reasoning).
- **You're billed for all thinking, whatever you see.** `usage.output_tokens_details.thinking_tokens` reports it, and `output_tokens` includes it.

```python
import os
import anthropic

client = anthropic.Anthropic(api_key=os.environ["ANTHROPIC_API_KEY"])

response = client.messages.create(
    model="claude-sonnet-5-5",
    max_tokens=16000,  # hard cap on thinking + answer combined
    thinking={"type": "adaptive", "display": "summarized"},  # ask for readable summaries
    output_config={"effort": "high"},  # soft guidance: how readily and deeply to think
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": "A bat and a ball cost $1.10 in total. "
               "The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much is the ball?"}],
)

if response.stop_reason == "refusal":  # declined: there may be no usable content
    raise SystemExit(f"refused: {response.stop_details}")
if response.stop_reason == "max_tokens":  # thinking can eat the whole cap
    print("warning: truncated; raise max_tokens or lower effort")

for block in response.content:  # read blocks by type, never by position
    if block.type == "thinking":
        print("[thinking summary]", block.thinking)  # "" when display is "omitted"
    elif block.type == "redacted_thinking":
        print("[redacted thinking: encrypted, pass it back unchanged]")
    elif block.type == "text":
        print("[answer]", block.text)

details = getattr(response.usage, "output_tokens_details", None)
thinking_tokens = details.thinking_tokens if details else None
print("billed output tokens:", response.usage.output_tokens, "thinking:", thinking_tokens)
```

### Interleaved thinking with tool use

An agent calls a tool, reads the result and decides what to do next ([§10](10-tool-use.md), [§11](11-agents.md)). *Interleaved thinking* lets the model reason after each tool result rather than only at the start of its turn:

```text
user:       "Did yesterday's load drop rows between staging and orders?"
assistant:  [thinking] → [tool_use: count(orders_staging)]
user:       [tool_result: 1204331]
assistant:  [thinking: "now the target"] → [tool_use: count(orders)]
user:       [tool_result: 1198004]
assistant:  [thinking: "6,327 missing; check the filter"] → [text: answer]
            └─────────── one assistant turn, one thinking mode ───────────┘
```

With adaptive thinking, interleaving is automatic **(GA)**; older manual-budget models needed the `interleaved-thinking-2025-05-14` beta header. Two rules follow. First, a tool loop is one assistant turn, so you must send every thinking block back with its `tool_use` block, complete and unmodified; modified blocks get a 400. Second, keep history append-only. Since Claude Fable 5.1, a thinking block stays valid only while the system prompt, tools and earlier messages are unchanged, and the API enforces this by default for accounts created on or after Aug 31, 2026 ([§16](16-context-engineering.md) and [§19](19-harness-engineering.md) cover the alternatives to editing history).

```python
import os
import anthropic

client = anthropic.Anthropic(api_key=os.environ["ANTHROPIC_API_KEY"])
tools = [{
    "name": "get_row_count",
    "description": "Return the number of rows in a warehouse table.",
    "input_schema": {"type": "object",
                     "properties": {"table": {"type": "string"}},
                     "required": ["table"]},
}]

def run_tool(name, args):  # the harness executes; the model only asks (P6)
    counts = {"orders_staging": "1204331", "orders": "1198004"}
    return counts.get(args["table"], "unknown table")

messages = [{"role": "user", "content": "Did yesterday's load drop rows between "
             "orders_staging and orders? Check both tables."}]
while True:
    response = client.messages.create(
        model="claude-sonnet-5-5", max_tokens=16000,
        output_config={"effort": "medium"}, tools=tools, messages=messages,
    )
    # Append the WHOLE content list: thinking blocks and signatures go back unchanged.
    messages.append({"role": "assistant", "content": response.content})
    if response.stop_reason != "tool_use":
        break
    results = [{"type": "tool_result", "tool_use_id": b.id, "content": run_tool(b.name, b.input)}
               for b in response.content if b.type == "tool_use"]
    messages.append({"role": "user", "content": results})  # all results in ONE user message

print(next((b.text for b in response.content if b.type == "text"), ""))
```

On Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1, the notes the model writes between tool calls also come back as `thinking` blocks. A UI that renders only `text` blocks goes quiet mid-task.

### Summarized vs raw thinking

No major closed vendor shows the raw chain of thought. Anthropic returns a summary written by a *different* model, or nothing, and on the newest Claude models a prompt that tries to extract the internal reasoning into the response text may be declined with `stop_details.category: "reasoning_extraction"`. OpenAI offers opt-in summaries (`reasoning.summary`) and Google offers opt-in thought summaries, each with an encrypted continuity token. Open-weight models such as R1 show the raw `<think>` text, because you run the weights.

There are three reasons to hide it. Traces are prime distillation data. Vendors want to monitor reasoning without training it to *look* good: OpenAI researchers (Baker et al., Mar 2025) found that strong optimization pressure on the chain of thought taught models to hide their intent, and suggested showing users a summary written by a separate model instead. And a trace isn't a faithful explanation anyway. In Anthropic's Apr 2025 tests, Claude 3.7 Sonnet used planted hints but mentioned them only 25% of the time, and DeepSeek-R1 39%. Models that exploited planted reward hacks on more than 99% of prompts verbalized them less than 2% of the time in most environments. Treat summaries as a debugging aid, not an audit log.

### Failure modes

- **Overthinking:** easy requests get slow answers. Lower the effort.
- **Truncation inside thinking:** a small `max_tokens` runs out mid-reasoning, giving `stop_reason: "max_tokens"` and little or no text.
- **Reward hacking leaking into behavior:** RL rewards passing the checker, not your intent, so coding models sometimes special-case tests or report unrun work as done. Keep the checker separate from the maker (P8).
- **Stale CoT-era prompts:** "show your reasoning step by step" duplicates work and can trigger `reasoning_extraction` declines.

### Cost and latency (P10)

Thinking tokens are billed as output, the expensive kind. At `claude-sonnet-5-5`'s $10 per million output tokens (Oct 2026 list price), a 300-token answer costs $0.003; behind 8,000 thinking tokens it costs about $0.083, roughly 28× more. Thinking tokens are also *decode* tokens, the slow sequential phase from §03. At 100 tokens per second, 8,000 of them is 80 seconds before the first visible word. Three more effects:

- `display: "omitted"` cuts time to first text token when streaming, but doesn't cut cost.
- Prior turns' thinking stays in context on current models, billed as input, and changing top-level effort between requests invalidates the prompt cache (per-message effort **(Preview)** avoids that).
- Judge cost per *completed task*: a cheap request that needs three retries isn't cheap.

### When to use a reasoning model, and when not

In 2026, "reasoning model or not" has mostly become "which effort level", since one hybrid model carries the dial. Use more thinking for multi-step logic, debugging, planning, code changes, agent loops and costly mistakes. Use low effort, or a non-thinking route, for classification, extraction, lookups, chat, voice ([§25](25-voice-agents.md)) and high-volume batch work. Thinking can't supply missing knowledge (P5): a model that doesn't know your schema reasons confidently toward a wrong answer, so ground it ([§09](09-rag.md)). Start at the default, measure on evals ([§26](26-evals.md)), and step effort down until quality drops.

### Why reasoning plus RL made agents reliable in 2025

Errors compound (P8). At 90% per-step reliability, a 20-step task succeeds 0.9^20 ≈ 12% of the time; at 99%, 0.99^20 ≈ 82%. In 2025 the RL recipe moved from puzzles to agentic work. OpenAI said it "trained both models to use tools through reinforcement learning" when it launched o3 and o4-mini (Apr 16, 2025). Training environments became multi-step tasks with checkable outcomes, and interleaved thinking let models check each tool result and recover: R1's backtracking, applied to tool loops. METR (Mar 2025) found that the length of tasks frontier models finish with 50% success had doubled roughly every seven months since 2019, driven primarily by "greater reliability and ability to adapt to mistakes". TechCrunch (Sep 16, 2025), citing The Information, reported that Anthropic leaders had discussed spending over $1B on RL environments over the following year (secondary). The bitter lesson (P12) applies too: scaffolding that forced models to plan step by step became dead weight once they planned inside their thinking. [§11](11-agents.md) picks up this thread.

## Comparison

**Three ways to spend test-time compute**

| Approach | How it works | Strength | Weakness | When to choose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chain-of-thought prompting (2022) | Examples or "think step by step" make the model write steps in its visible answer | No training needed; works on any capable model | Imitated, not learned; early errors propagate; redundant on reasoning models | Small or non-reasoning open models |
| RL-trained reasoning (o1, R1, Claude thinking) | RL on verifiable rewards taught the model to think in a separate channel first | Learned self-checking; one effort dial | Cost and latency; traces aren't faithful; overthinks easy inputs | Multi-step problems, coding, agents |
| Parallel sampling (self-consistency, best-of-N) | N independent samples, then a vote or a verifier picks | Wall-clock time near one sample; agreement rate signals confidence | N× cost; needs comparable answers or a verifier | Short, comparable answers where accuracy matters most |

**Thinking controls by vendor (Oct 2026)**

| Control | Anthropic Messages | OpenAI Responses | Google Gemini Interactions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Depth dial | `output_config.effort`: `low` … `max` | `reasoning.effort`: `none` … `max` (model-dependent); `reasoning.mode: "pro"` on some models | `generation_config.thinking_level`: `minimal` … `high` (model-dependent) |
| Off switch | Model-specific (`disabled`, `between_tools`, or none) | `effort: "none"` on some models | Lowest level `minimal` on some models; Google says it doesn't guarantee thinking is off |
| Hard cap | `max_tokens` (thinking + text) | `max_output_tokens` | `max_output_tokens` (includes thoughts) |
| What you see | Summary or empty (`display`); never raw | Opt-in `reasoning.summary`; never raw | Opt-in `thinking_summaries` |
| Continuity token | `signature` per thinking block | `encrypted_content` or stored state | Thought signatures |
| Usage field | `output_tokens_details.thinking_tokens` | `output_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens` | `total_thought_tokens` |

## Check your understanding

**Q: Why does writing intermediate steps let a transformer solve problems it cannot solve when forced to answer in its first token?**

Each generated token gets one fixed-cost forward pass and is fed back as input (P1). Writing steps buys more serial computation and a scratchpad the model can attend to. The result is better accuracy on multi-step problems, at the price of tokens, cost and latency (P10). It doesn't help when the bottleneck is missing knowledge rather than computation.

**Q: Why did RL with verifiable rewards produce backtracking and self-checking when chain-of-thought prompting did not?**

Prompted CoT imitates the form of reasoning, and nothing rewards it for being correct. RLVR scores only whether the final answer passes a programmatic check, so any habit that raises the hit rate gets reinforced. It only works where answers can be checked automatically, and it rewards passing the checker rather than meeting intent. It's the wrong tool for open-ended tasks with no verifier.

**Q: GRPO vs PPO: what does GRPO drop, and what does it use instead?**

PPO trains a separate value model to estimate a baseline. GRPO drops it and uses the mean (and spread) of rewards across a group of sampled answers to the same prompt. That saves a second large model's memory and compute, which makes RL on big models cheaper. The cost is more generation per training step, since it needs several samples per prompt.

**Q: Serial vs parallel test-time compute: when would you prefer majority voting over raising the effort level?**

Voting runs N independent samples, so wall-clock time stays near one sample and the agreement rate doubles as a confidence score. It suits short, comparable answers such as a number or a label, when you can afford N× the cost. For long free-form outputs, where no two samples match, raise effort instead.

**Q: What would happen if your code read only `content[0].text` from a Claude response with adaptive thinking on?**

The first block is often a `thinking` block, which has no `text` field and an empty `thinking` field by default. The code would crash or read nothing, and because the model decides per request whether to think, the bug would show up only some of the time. Always iterate over the blocks and select by `type`.

**Q: Why should you not treat a thinking summary as an audit log of why the model made a decision?**

A different model writes the summary, from a trace that is itself often unfaithful: in Anthropic's tests, models used planted hints but usually didn't mention them. Relying on summaries for compliance or root-cause analysis gives false confidence. Use them to debug prompts, and verify behavior with evals and independent checks (P8).

**Q: Why did RL post-training on agentic tasks make 2025 agents reliable enough to ship?**

Success compounds as p^n (P8), so raising per-step reliability from 0.9 to 0.99 lifts a 20-step task from about 12% to about 82%. RL on tool-using tasks with checkable outcomes, plus interleaved thinking, taught models to check results and recover from mistakes. The price was longer, costlier runs that still need outside verification. For short, well-defined tasks, a plain workflow is still cheaper (P7).

## Takeaways

- Fixed compute per token means the only way to "think harder" is to generate more tokens (P1, P10).
- CoT prompting exploited this by imitation; RL with verifiable rewards turned it into a trained skill.
- o1 (Sep 2024) showed reasoning scales with train- and test-time compute; DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025) published an open recipe: rule-based rewards plus GRPO.
- On current Claude models, use adaptive thinking plus `output_config.effort`; `budget_tokens` is rejected and `max_tokens` is the only hard cap.
- Read responses by block type, return thinking blocks unchanged, and keep history append-only.
- You pay for all thinking whether or not you see it, and what you see is a summary, not a faithful explanation.
- RL on agentic tasks raised per-step reliability, and p^n turned that into usable agents.
